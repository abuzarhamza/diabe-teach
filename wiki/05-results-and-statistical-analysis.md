## The Metacognitive Shift: "I Don't Know" vs. Blind Guessing

A critical pedagogical breakthrough in this study is the behavior around uncertain questions:

```
Post-test Error Comparison:
Control Group:     [25.7% Wrong Guesses  ] [5.4% Admitted Unknown]
Diabe-teach Group: [ 9.3% Wrong Guesses  ] [8.9% Admitted Unknown]
```

- **Control Group Behavior:** When students engaged in traditional solo self-study, they exhibited overconfidence and a strong tendency to guess blindly. More than one in four answers ($25.7\%$) on the post-test was an incorrect distractor.
- **Diabe-teach Group Behavior:** Students in the game-based group committed less than a third as many errors ($9.3\%$). At the same time, their rate of answering *"I don't know"* remained significantly higher ($8.9\%$ vs. $5.4\%$, $p < 0.001$).
- **Clinical Relevance:** In clinical practice, unreflective guessing can directly compromise patient safety and lead to diagnostic errors. Recognizing the boundaries of one's own clinical mastery is foundational to reflective, safe medical practice.
