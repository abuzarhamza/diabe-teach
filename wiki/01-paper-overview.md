## Abstract Summary

### Background
The global escalation of diabetes mellitus demands that medical undergraduates build a nuanced, multi-faceted understanding of its pathophysiology, clinical features, diagnostic workups, and management strategies. Conventional didactic lectures and text reading often lack interactive stimuli that maximize cognitive engagement, collaborative clinical discussions, and knowledge retention.

### Objective
To evaluate the pedagogical efficacy of "Diabe-teach"—a newly designed educational board game—on preclinical medical students' knowledge retention and comprehension of diabetes mellitus in comparison to structured conventional self-study.

### Design & Setting
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial (RCT).
- **Participants:** 56 preclinical female medical students (Years 1 to 3) at Dubai Medical College for Girls (DMCG), randomly allocated 1:1 into:
  1. **Intervention Group ($n = 28$):** Diabe-teach board game session (orientation, trial round, two 30-minute gameplay rounds).
  2. **Control Group ($n = 28$):** Supervised self-study with identical instructional handouts and digital materials.
- **Duration:** Structured 2-hour session (15 min pretest, 75 min intervention/study, 15 min post-test, 15 min feedback survey).

### Key Results
1. **Knowledge Acquisition:** Both groups demonstrated statistically significant improvements from baseline pretest to post-test ($p < 0.001$).
   - Control group: $25.0\% \to 68.9\%$
   - Diabe-teach group: $27.9\% \to 81.8\%$ (significantly higher post-test score than control, $p < 0.001$, $t = -3.93$).
2. **Metacognitive Self-Awareness (Error Reduction vs. Gap Awareness):**
   - The Diabe-teach group showed markedly fewer wrong guesses on post-tests ($9.3\%$ vs. $25.7\%$ in control, $p < 0.001$).
   - The Diabe-teach group had a significantly higher rate of selecting *"I don't know"* when unsure ($8.9\%$ vs. $5.4\%$, $p < 0.001$), demonstrating an increased willingness to recognize true knowledge boundaries rather than blind guessing.
3. **Student Perception & Acceptability:**
   - $100\%$ agreed that Diabe-teach boosted confidence in discussing complex concepts.
   - $100\%$ agreed it improved their ability to correlate disparate pieces of clinical information.
   - $100\%$ expressed a clear preference for Diabe-teach over traditional self-study.
   - $0\%$ experienced anxiety or stress during gameplay.
   - $100\%$ recommended Diabe-teach for future medical courses.

## Significance to Medical Education & Healthcare

1. **Non-Trivia Pedagogical Format:** Unlike popular medical board games or quiz bowl apps that test binary recall (single question $\to$ single answer), Diabe-teach uses an open-ended associative mechanism. Students must connect terms and verbally justify therapeutic or pathological linkages.
2. **High Replayability & Non-Monotony:** With 49 terms shuffled dynamically across a 7×7 matrix, no two board layouts or valid chain opportunities are identical.
3. **Metacognition in Clinical Practice:** In medicine, blind guessing can lead to fatal diagnostic errors. Fostering self-awareness (knowing what you do not know) is as essential as rote knowledge. Diabe-teach significantly minimized unreflective guessing.
4. **Low Tech Barrier / High Accessibility:** The physical game requires only printed card stock and pen/paper, making it universally deployable across resource-constrained environments.
